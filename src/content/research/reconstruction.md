Reconstruction connects what a detector records to the physics of an event. My current work at the Institute of High Energy Physics develops a robust machine learning framework for atmospheric neutrino reconstruction at JUNO.

Earlier, I worked on particle reconstruction in the Super Fine-Grained Detector (SuperFGD), part of the upgraded T2K near detector. I developed a trackless pion reconstruction technique to reach particles below the usual tracking threshold, and adapted an elastically scattered and contained proton selection to improve momentum and transverse kinematic imbalance reconstruction.

That work also involved the detector itself. I contributed to SuperFGD assembly, trained as a data-acquisition expert, and carried out hardware fixes. Working across simulation, reconstruction and detector operations informs how I approach the reliability of analysis methods.

The public Neutrino 2024 poster and Neutel 2023 presentation describe the T2K reconstruction and detector work.
