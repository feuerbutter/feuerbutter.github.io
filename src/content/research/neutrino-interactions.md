Neutrino experiments infer the properties of an incoming neutrino from the particles left behind in a detector. Interactions inside nuclei complicate that connection. My work studies observables and models that help disentangle the underlying interaction from the effects of the nucleus.

During my DPhil, I used the GENIE Comparisons framework to develop a combined tune to transverse kinematic imbalance measurements with and without pion production. I investigated how a matched choice of data and model parameters can improve the description of these complementary measurements.

I also devised centre-of-momentum variables for charged-current events containing a pion and a proton. These observables probe final-state interactions while reducing dependence on the initial nuclear state and the neutrino flux.

A related strand of my T2K work explored heavy neutral leptons: I interfaced the BeamHNL generator with the near-detector simulation, developed signal selections, and used the CLs method to study sensitivity.
